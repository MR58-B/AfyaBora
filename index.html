1. The Fundamentals

Understanding HIV, AIDS & the Immune System

Learn the difference between HIV and AIDS, how the virus targets CD4 cells, and the three clinical stages of infection.

THE VIRUS

What is HIV?

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is a virus that attacks the body's immune system, particularly CD4 cells (T-helper cells), which help the body fight infections and diseases.

If untreated, HIV gradually damages the immune system. With proper medical treatment (ART), a person can live with HIV for many healthy, productive years.

Two Main Types of HIV

  • HIV-1: The most common type worldwide; accounts for most infections.
  • HIV-2: Less common and found mainly in West Africa, though it also occurs in other regions.
ADVANCED STAGE

What is AIDS?

AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) is the most advanced stage of HIV infection. It occurs when HIV has severely weakened the immune system, making the body vulnerable to serious opportunistic infections and certain cancers.

Important: A person diagnosed with HIV does not automatically have AIDS. With effective ART, many people living with HIV never develop AIDS.

Key Immune Indicators

  • CD4 Cells: White blood cells that coordinate immunity and indicate immune health.
  • Viral Load: Amount of HIV in the blood (High, Low, or Undetectable).

Key Differences: HIV vs. AIDS

Aspect HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome)

The 3 Stages & Signs of HIV Infection

Remember: You cannot tell if someone has HIV by appearance or symptoms alone—an HIV test is necessary.

Stage 1: Acute HIV Infection (Approx. 2–4 Weeks After Infection)

High levels of HIV are present in the blood, creating a high risk of transmission as the immune system begins responding. Some people develop flu-like symptoms while others have none:

Fever Headache Sore throat Skin rash Swollen lymph nodes Muscle & joint pain Night sweats Fatigue Mouth ulcers

Note: These symptoms are not specific to HIV and can be caused by many other illnesses.

Stage 2: Chronic HIV Infection (Clinical Latency)

  • The virus remains active in the body, reproducing usually at lower levels.
  • The person may feel healthy and have no noticeable symptoms for years.
  • Without treatment, the immune system gradually becomes weaker over time.
  • With effective ART, people can live healthy lives for decades without progressing to AIDS.

Stage 3: AIDS (Advanced HIV Infection)

Occurs when severe immune system damage leaves the body vulnerable to opportunistic infections and certain cancers. Requires prompt medical assessment and treatment:

Severe weight loss Persistent fever Chronic diarrhoea Recurrent infections Persistent swollen lymph nodes Prolonged cough Oral thrush Severe fatigue Tuberculosis (TB) Certain cancers
2. Know the Facts

How HIV Is—and Is NOT—Transmitted

Clear scientific facts protect individuals and eliminate unfounded fear in homes, schools, and workplaces.

How HIV IS Transmitted

HIV spreads only when specific infected body fluids—blood, semen, pre-seminal fluid, vaginal fluids, rectal fluids, or breast milk—enter another person's bloodstream or contact mucous membranes or damaged tissue:

A. Sexual Transmission Vaginal or anal sex without condoms, PrEP, or sustained viral suppression (U=U). Other STIs increase acquisition risk.
B. Sharing Contaminated Needles Sharing needles, syringes, or unsterilised injecting/cutting equipment exposed to infected blood.
C. Mother-to-Child Transmission During pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding—greatly reduced with proper maternal ART and infant care.
D. Contaminated Blood Receiving infected blood products (very rare in Kenya's regulated, screened blood supply).

How HIV is NOT Transmitted

HIV is never spread through ordinary everyday social interaction. You cannot get HIV from:

Shaking hands
Hugging
Sharing food
Sharing plates, cups or spoons
Using the same toilet
Sharing swimming pools
Sitting next to someone
Mosquito bites
Sweat or tears
Coughing or sneezing
Working together
Studying together
3. Knowing Your Status

HIV Testing & Window Period Estimator

Only an HIV test can reliably determine your status. Understand test types, detection windows, and use our interactive estimator below.

A. Rapid HIV Test

Uses a small finger-prick blood sample; results are available in minutes. Reactive results require confirmation under Kenya's approved testing algorithm.

B. Antibody Test

Detects antibodies produced by the body's immune system in response to HIV infection.

C. Antigen/Antibody Test

Detects both HIV antibodies and the p24 antigen, identifying infection earlier than antibody-only tests.

D. Nucleic Acid Test (NAT)

Detects HIV genetic material directly in the blood. Identifies infection earliest of all test types.

Typical HIV Window Periods by Test Type

The window period is the time between acquiring HIV and when a test can reliably detect it. A negative result immediately after exposure may require retesting after the window period.

Test Type Typical Detection Window
Nucleic Acid Test (NAT)10 – 33 days
Laboratory Antigen/Antibody Test18 – 45 days
Rapid Finger-Prick Antigen/Antibody Test18 – 90 days
Antibody-Only Test23 – 90 days

4. Effective Treatment

Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) & U=U

While there is currently no widely available cure, daily ART controls HIV, restores immunity, and prevents sexual transmission.

U = U

Undetectable = Untransmittable

A person living with HIV who takes effective treatment as prescribed and maintains an undetectable viral load does not transmit HIV to their sexual partners.

Important: U=U requires consistent daily ART and regular viral load monitoring. U=U applies specifically to sexual transmission—it should not be taken as proof that HIV cannot be transmitted through sharing injecting equipment or breastfeeding without clinical guidance.

What Does ART Do?

  • Reduces the amount of HIV (viral load) in the blood.
  • Helps the immune system and CD4 cells recover.
  • Reduces the risk of opportunistic infections.
  • Helps prevent progression to AIDS.
  • Helps people living with HIV live longer, healthier lives.
  • Prevents sexual transmission when viral suppression is maintained.

Common First-Line Adult Treatment in Kenya: TLD

Kenya's standard first-line adult combination is TDF + 3TC + DTG (TLD)—combining Tenofovir, Lamivudine, and Dolutegravir. Exact regimens are selected by qualified clinicians under NASCOP guidelines. Never stop or change dosage without medical advice.

Common Antiretroviral (ARV) Medicines

Medicine Function
Tenofovir (TDF)Prevents HIV from making genetic copies.
Lamivudine (3TC)Interferes with viral replication.
Dolutegravir (DTG)Blocks an enzyme HIV needs to reproduce.
Efavirenz (EFV)Interferes with HIV replication.
Abacavir (ABC)Interferes with viral genetic copying.
Zidovudine (AZT)Prevents HIV from reproducing effectively.
5. Stay Protected

Comprehensive HIV Prevention

Combine proven medical and behavioural tools to protect yourself and your partners.

ACT WITHIN 72 HOURS

Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

Emergency medication taken by HIV-negative people after possible exposure (condom breakage, sexual assault, needle-stick injury, or sharing needles).

  • Start as soon as possible—ideally within 24 hours and no later than 72 hours.
  • Taken daily for 28 days.
  • Do not wait for symptoms—go to a Kenyan hospital or health facility immediately.
BEFORE EXPOSURE

Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

Preventive medication taken by HIV-negative people at substantial risk of acquiring HIV before exposure.

  • Available as daily oral PrEP, long-acting injectable PrEP, or other approved formulations under Kenyan guidelines.
  • Requires an HIV-negative test and medical assessment before starting.
PROVEN METHODS

Condoms, VMMC, Sterile Needles & PMTCT

  • Condoms: Correct, consistent male or female condom use prevents HIV, STIs, and unintended pregnancy.
  • Regular HIV Testing: Know your status early.
  • Sterile Needles: Never share needles or unsterilised equipment.
  • Medical Male Circumcision (VMMC): Reduces heterosexual HIV acquisition risk in men (partial protection; pair with condoms).
  • PMTCT: Maternal ART and infant prophylaxis protect babies from mother-to-child transmission.
6. Holistic Health & Dignity

Living Well with HIV: Family, Nutrition, Mental Health & Rights

People living with HIV can build families, eat nourishing local foods, prevent complications, and thrive free from discrimination.

Relationships, Pregnancy & Breastfeeding

Serodifferent (Serodiscordant) Couples: Couples where one partner has HIV and the other does not can enjoy healthy marriages and relationships using ART (U=U), PrEP for the HIV-negative partner, condoms, and open communication.

Pregnancy & Breastfeeding Care in Kenya:

  1. Early antenatal HIV testing and regular clinic visits.
  2. Starting or continuing ART and monitoring maternal viral load.
  3. Following medical advice during childbirth and infant prophylaxis/testing.
  4. Following qualified Kenyan provider guidance on breastfeeding and infant feeding—never make independent feeding or medication changes without clinical advice.

Nutrition & Food Hygiene

Good nutrition supports strength and immunity alongside ART (supplements never replace prescribed ARVs):

  • Fruits & Vegetables: Bananas, oranges, mangoes, avocados, spinach, sukuma wiki, carrots, and cabbage.
  • Proteins & Dairy: Eggs, beans, fish, chicken, lentils, milk, and yoghurt.
  • Carbohydrates & Healthy Fats: Rice, potatoes, maize, sweet potatoes, nuts, and seeds.

Food Safety: Drink clean safe water, wash produce thoroughly, cook food well, and consult a clinician for weight loss or persistent diarrhoea.

When immunity is weakened without treatment, people may develop opportunistic infections—such as Tuberculosis (TB), oral thrush, pneumonia, cryptococcal meningitis, herpes zoster (shingles), toxoplasmosis, Kaposi's sarcoma, and chronic diarrhoeal infections—as well as neurological, kidney, liver (especially with Hepatitis B/C), lymphoma, or cardiovascular complications. Effective ART and routine monitoring prevent most of these risks.

Emotional Wellbeing: Depression, anxiety, stress, and fear of rejection can affect people living with HIV. Confidential counselling, peer support groups, self-acceptance, and family support promote resilience and treatment adherence.

Fighting Stigma & Discrimination: Denying someone employment, education, social inclusion, or breaching medical confidentiality is unfair and harmful. HIV is a manageable medical condition—every person deserves dignity, respect, privacy, and equal opportunities.

Emergency PEP (Within 72 Hours)

If you think you may have been exposed to HIV (condom breakage, sexual assault, needle-stick injury, or sharing needles):

  1. Go to a Kenyan hospital, health centre, or CCC immediately—ideally within 24 hours and no later than 72 hours.
  2. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.
  3. A healthcare provider will perform a rapid HIV test and prescribe a 28-day course of PEP if eligible.
  4. Take all 28 days of medication exactly as prescribed and attend follow-up testing.

12 Important Facts Everyone Should Remember About HIV
  1. HIV attacks the immune system, particularly CD4 cells.
  2. AIDS is the most advanced stage of HIV infection.
  3. HIV is transmitted through specific infected body fluids.
  4. HIV cannot be transmitted through ordinary social contact.
  5. HIV testing is the only reliable way to know someone's HIV status.
  6. There is effective treatment for HIV.
  7. ART helps people living with HIV live long and healthy lives.
  8. People who maintain an undetectable viral load do not transmit HIV sexually (U=U).
  9. PrEP helps prevent HIV before possible exposure.
  10. PEP helps prevent HIV after possible exposure and must be started within 72 hours.
  11. Pregnant women living with HIV can have HIV-negative babies with appropriate treatment and care.
  12. People living with HIV deserve love, respect, privacy and equal treatment.
